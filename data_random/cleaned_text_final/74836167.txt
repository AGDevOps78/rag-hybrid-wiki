Maibarol (ꯃꯥꯏꯕꯔꯣꯜ), also known as Maibaron (ꯃꯥꯏꯕꯔꯣꯟ),[1] is a traditional Meitei language term that refers to the art and science of therapeutic practice. It includes a variety of healing methods such as spiritual rituals, herbal medicine, pulse diagnosis, and even crude surgical techniques of Meitei civilization. Practitioners of this ancient medical system were known as Maiba (male) and Maibi (female),[2] but in earlier times they were also called Ametpa, which literally means "masseur" but colloquially means "therapist."[3][4][5] The organized institution for therapeutic practice in Manipur was called Ametpa Loishang (ꯑꯃꯦꯠꯄ ꯂꯣꯏꯁꯪ), tracing back its origin from the time of King Nongda Lairen Pakhangba (ꯅꯣꯡꯗꯥ ꯂꯥꯏꯔꯦꯟ ꯄꯥꯈꯪꯕ), in 33 CE.[6] According to royal chronicles, the Ametpa Loishang was officially, formally and systematically established around Over time, the methods of healing used by Ametpa Loishang evolved: One significant record from the royal chronicle dates to June 19, The expertise of Meitei therapeutic practitioners was recognized beyond the region. The knowledge and practices of the Ametpa Loishang contributed to both community health and the cultural heritage of the Meitei people. Though modern medicine has replaced many traditional methods, the Maiba-Maibi system remains a respected part of Meitei identity and history.[3][4][7]

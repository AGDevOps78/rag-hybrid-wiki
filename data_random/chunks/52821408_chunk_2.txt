One of the most important techniques is X-ray crystallography, which is used to analyze solids.[6][7] Another dominant technique is NMR spectroscopy, which is routinely used in organic chemistry but is applicable to a variety of sample types.[8] In addition to static structures, structural chemistry also includes a focus on dynamics, such as fluxionality. In such cases, the time scale of the technique must be matched with the time scale of the dynamics. A classic example is dimethylformamide (DMF) where the dynamics are analyzed by dynamic NMR spectroscopy.[9]

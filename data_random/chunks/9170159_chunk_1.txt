The sensation of depth in motion parallax is very similar to the sensation of depth in horizontal disparity. In both cases, stereopsis occurs, where people and objects at different distances appear "separate" from each other. Although relative distances can be estimated very precisely, this is not true for the distance to the observer, which is not perceived as precisely. The horopter has been used to describe the relation between disparity and stereopsis, and between disparity and double images. It depicts points with zero disparity relative to the fixation point (α = β). Horizontal disparity, and in some cases also vertical disparity,[3] can contribute to stereopsis, but only if horizontal, vertical and cyclo disparities are limited in size. Horizontal disparity is also associated with the separation perceived between so-called double images. Zero disparities and small disparities are associated with seeing fused images in directional vision.

He lectured regularly at the Chicago School of Psychology, wrote many articles for The Hypnotic Magazine and Suggestion, and conducted joint experiments with Dr. Parkyn on hypnotic and suggestive phenomena. During the same period he chaired the Psychological Section of the Medico-Legal Society of New York, was the extension lecturer for the University of Chicago, and served as professor of morbid psychology at the Chicago Post-Graduate College of Medicine. His involvement linked the school's work to current medical and medico-legal research and helped secure its reputation as a serious scientific institution.[200][16][201][17][202] Among some the school's most prominent graduates involved in the New Thought movement were Stanley Lefevre Krebs, William Walker Atkinson, Sydney B. Flower, and E. Virgil Neal.

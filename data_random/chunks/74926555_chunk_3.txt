When the temperature reaches 1,000 °C, the CO2  reacts with the unburned carbon to create CO:  CO2 + C ⇌ 2 CO when T > 1 000 °C (Boudouard reaction) The production of H2 cannot be achieved by the thermal decomposition of water, as the temperatures involved are too low. Hydrogen is in fact produced along with carbon monoxide by the reaction: H2O + C → H2 + CO when T > 1 000 °C These two reducing gas production reactions, which consume 172.45 and 131.4 kJ/mol  respectively, are highly endothermic and operate by limiting charge heating.

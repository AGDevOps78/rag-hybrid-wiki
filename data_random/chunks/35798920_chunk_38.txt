In traditional Hopi marriages, a woman's family paid a dowry in cornmeal to the groom's family. In the northeast, individuals generally made their own marital decisions. Couples were freely allowed to part ways. People in the Innu society enjoyed a lot of sexual freedom. Legitimacy of children was not much of a concern, because most things belonged to the entire tribe. Among the Aborigines in Australia, marriage was strictly managed by elders. Girls were typically assigned a husband during their childhoods. By the Edo period, the expectations of filial loyalty during marriage had grown even stronger.

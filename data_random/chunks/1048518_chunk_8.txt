Supramolecular metallocycles and metallacrowns are related components. Common metallocycle shapes in these types of applications include triangles, squares, and pentagons, each bearing functional groups that connect the pieces via "self-assembly."  Many supramolecular systems require their components to have suitable spacing and conformations relative to each other, and therefore easily employed structural units are required. Supramolecular chemistry per se has found few applications, but underpins some useful phenomena. The concepts of supramolecular chemistry can inspire the design of catalyst. Non-covalent interactions influence the binding reactants. Design based on supramolecular chemistry has inspired the design of functional biomaterials and therapeutics. Supramolecular biomaterials afford a number of modular and generalizable platforms with tunable mechanical, chemical and biological properties. These include systems based on supramolecular assembly of peptides, host–guest macrocycles, high-affinity hydrogen bonding, and metal–ligand interactions.

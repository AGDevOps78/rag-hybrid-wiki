As another example, if we consider the pair (u, v) = (10, 12), then u + v = 22 and v + u∗ = 2; and we will have ± 6 and ± 8 even bone numbers at our disposal. Taking c = ±6, we have the sum u + v + c to be 28 and 16. While 28 does not fall within the sets D or S, 16 falls in set S. By inspection, we find that if (a, b) = (−7, − 9), then a + b = −16; and it will satisfy the first constraint equation. Also, taking f = ± 8, we have the sum v + u∗ + f to be 10 and -6. While 10 does not fall within the sets D or S, −6 falls in set D. Since −7 and −9 have already been assigned to a and b, clearly (d, e) = (-5, 11) so that d + e = 6; and it will satisfy the second constraint equation. Likewise, taking c = ±8, we have the sum u + v + c to be 30 and 14.

[citation needed] Inadequate coordination between the health care providers and diabetes patients is a major barrier to properly implementing the care guidelines.[30] Lack of collaboration and coordination leads to information conflict affecting the quality of self-management.[30] The diabetes patients who were identified to develop healthy diabetes management habits had a supportive patient-provider relationship.[31] People from economically disadvantaged backgrounds can have limited access to care which is one reason for inadequate coordination between healthcare providers and patients.[31] Healthcare professionals play a crucial role in diabetes self-management. Diabetes patients rely on health care professionals to obtain information and support in developing an individualised self-management plan.[5] Self-management goals and recommendations given by health care professionals were identified to have a significant positive impact on the eating habits and physical activity of diabetes patients. Lack of proper emphasis on self-management may suggest to diabetes patients that self-management is either less important or even unimportant for diabetes management.[5]

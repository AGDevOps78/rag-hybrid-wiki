Flyover was also criticized for its early imagery, as Apple was initially unable to properly capture smaller or lower objects or buildings, resulting in structures such as trees having a blocky or otherwise low-quality appearance. Flyover additionally had glitches whilst interacting with other Maps features, including road markings, which would tend to follow the tops of 3D buildings rather than remain focused on the designated road, which particularly became an issue for bridges. Google Earth, which also hosted 3D imagery on their mapping site, was noted to be experiencing similar issues with their image rendering. As a result of these issues, Flyover was initially criticized for its low quality, as well as being parodied online. The US embassy in Norway also stated its support for Apple in adding the feature. Outside of Apple, other companies including Nokia were permitted to survey Oslo for 3D imagery.

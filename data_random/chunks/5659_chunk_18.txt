Hence, various methods will give similar, but slightly different results.[97] Along each row of the periodic table, the radius tends to decrease from the alkali metal column to the noble gases. This is caused by the increasing attraction of the nuclear charge overcoming the mutual repulsion of the electrons as a shell is filled. A smaller radius means the atom is less chemically reactive, as the valence electrons are drawn closer to the nucleus.[98] Depending on the type of chemical bond, the atomic radius determines the atomic separation in a crystal, and hence the mass density.[99] When an element has allotropes with different densities, one representative allotrope is typically selected in summary presentations,[citation needed] while densities for each allotrope can be stated where more detail is provided. For example, the three familiar allotropes of carbon (amorphous carbon, graphite, and diamond) have densities of 1.8–2.1, 2.267, and 3.515 g/cm3, respectively.[100] The various chemical elements are formally identified by their unique atomic numbers, their accepted names, and their chemical symbols. The known elements have atomic numbers from 1 to 118, conventionally presented as Arabic numerals. Since the elements can be uniquely sequenced by atomic number, conventionally from lowest to highest (as in a periodic table), sets of elements are sometimes specified by such notation as "through", "beyond", or "from ... through", as in "through iron", "beyond uranium", or "from lanthanum through lutetium".

For instance, people with complete androgen insensitivity syndrome (CAIS) have low bone density as a result of "decreased circulating estrogen and skeletal resistance to androgen action."[34] Some intersex people no longer produce their own sex hormones due to receiving a gonadectomy (surgical removal of the gonads).[34] This necessitates the use of long term hormone therapy to maintain bone health.[35] People with Turner syndrome, gonadal dysgenesis, Klinefelter syndrome and congenital adrenal hyperplasia also face issues with bone health, particularly osteopenia and osteoporosis.[36][37] In Klinefelter syndrome and gonadal dysgenesis, this is caused by insufficient sex hormones.[36] In CAH, it is due to excessive adrenal androgen as well as lifelong treatment with glucocorticoid, a type of steroid.[36][38][39] Bone health can be maintained through monitoring bone mineral density using dual-energy x-ray absorptiometry and providing hormone replacement therapy to prevent osteoporosis.[40][41][37] Hormone replacement serves additional purposes other than just maintaining bone health. In those with CAH, steroids called mineralocorticoids help regulate salt and water in the body to prevent salt-wasting (an imbalance of salt in the body).[42][43] Some intersex people need to be provided oestrogen or testosterone is needed to induce puberty.[44] This is needed to help the body mature and develop secondary sex characteristics, which allows intersex people to develop alongside their peers, and prevent delayed intellectual, social, and emotional development.[44] Some intersex variations are associated with an increased risk of cancer. Men with Klinefelter syndrome, especially those with mosaicism, are at a higher risk of dying from breast cancer than endosex men.[45] Those with Swyer syndrome are at an increased risk of developing cancer, specifically germ cell tumors, if atypical gonads are not removed.[40][46] Gonadoblastomas are precancerous lesions that predominantly form in intersex people with gonadal dysgenesis and a Y chromosome.[47][48] This includes conditions such as Swyer syndrome or Turner syndrome with Y chromosome mosaicism.[49][47] These cancer risks are generally addressed through genetic screening and a gonadectomy, if necessary.[47] The undescended testes of those with androgen insensitivity syndrome also pose a cancer risk. Those with complete androgen insensitivity syndrome have a 3.6 percent chance of developing a malignant tumor by age 25 and a 33 percent chance by age 50, though malignancy before adulthood is rare.[47][50] Partial androgen insensitivity syndrome poses a higher cancer risk if undescended testes are present.[50] The risk for germ cell tumors from undescended testes is 15 to 50 percent, but the risk for testes surgically moved to the scrotum is unknown.[47] Orchiectomy, removal of the testes, is a preventative treatment option for these cancer risks.[50] For those with partial androgen insensitivity syndrome who are raised male, a procedure to put the testes into the scrotum called an orchiopexy is done to lower the risk of malignancy.[50] Some intersex variations are associated with metabolic and cardiovascular conditions. Those with XY DSD are more likely to be born with heart defects.[13] Intersex variations in sex chromosomes are associated with an increased risk of both type one and type two diabetes.[13] Turner syndrome, one such chromosomal intersex variation, is also associated with hypertension.[41] 23 to 50 percent of those with Turner syndrome are born with congenital heart abnormalities.[51] Fetuses with confirmed or suspected Turner syndrome should receive a fetal echocardiogram.[51] Children born with Turner syndrome and accompanying heart abnormalities are recommended to receive care from a pediatric cardiologist.[51] Various intersex conditions coincide with kidney abnormalities. People with androgen insensitivity syndrome may have atypical kidneys or upper urinary tracts.[13] Hypospadias may also be related to urinary tract anomalies, though existing research is conflicting.[13][52] People with Herlyn-Werner-Wunderlich syndrome can be born with one kidney.[53] Urinary tract ultrasounds can be used to check for kidney abnormalities in those with Herlyn-Werner-Wunderlich syndrome.[53] Other types of anomalies may be present at birth. For example, most people born with campomelic dysplasia have micrognathism, a small lower jaw, and laryngomalacia, floppy cartilage in the larynx that obstructs one's airway.[54][55] Cleft palates are also present in most people born with the condition.[54] Cleft palates, severe laryngomalacia and micrognathia are treatable through surgery.[56][57][58] Fertility varies depending on the intersex variations one has. For instance, people with Turner syndrome can become pregnant with donor eggs or, more rarely, become pregnant unassisted; meanwhile, men with 46 XX are always infertile.[59] Fertility counseling can be provided by a clinician to address fertility complications caused by the patient's intersex variation and what treatment options are available.[60] Procedures such as testicular sperm extraction, in vitro fertilization, or receiving a uterus transplant can help some intersex people have children.[59] People with MRKH syndrome, a rare intersex condition causing an absent or underdeveloped vagina and uterus, can produce children with a surrogate and in-vitro fertilization of their own eggs since they usually have fully-formed ovaries.[61] Since Intracytoplasmic sperm injection, a type of in vitro fertilization in which sperm is directly injected into an egg cell, is another tool for assisted reproduction.[62] It can help people with 5-alpha-reductase type 2 deficiency or Klinefelter syndrome to have children.[59][63] It can also be used in some cases of ovotesticular DSD if the father is able to produce sperm and has not had his testes removed.[64] In-vitro fertilization with donor eggs allows people with a uterus but no ovaries to give birth, such as in some cases of Swyer syndrome.[65][40] Females with Turner syndrome lose their eggs at a fast rate compared to endosex females; before they are born, the majority of their egg cells are apoptopic, or in the process of dying.[66] Most people with Turner syndrome experience early menopause.[66] In some cases, cryopreservation is used to maintain reproductive options.

Some of these elements, notably bismuth (atomic number 83), thorium (atomic number 90), and uranium (atomic number 92), have one or more isotopes with half-lives long enough to survive from before the Solar System formed. The remaining longest-lived isotopes have half lives too short for them to have been present at the beginning of the Solar System, and are therefore "transient elements". Of these 11 transient elements, five (polonium, radon, radium, actinium, and protactinium) are relatively common decay products of thorium and uranium. The remaining six transient elements (technetium, promethium, astatine, francium, neptunium, and plutonium) occur only rarely,  as products of rare decay modes or nuclear reaction processes involving uranium or other heavy elements. The remaining 24 heaviest elements (those beyond plutonium, element 94) are radioactive, with half-lives so short that they are not found on Earth and must be synthesized. Five have been discovered in the spectrum of Przybylski's star, from element 95 (americium) to 99 (einsteinium). These are thought to be neutron capture products of uranium and thorium.

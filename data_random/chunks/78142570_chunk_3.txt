Rosa suggests this creates a "contraction of the present," described as "a decrease of the length of time for which there prevail secure expectations regarding the stability of the circumstances of action". As a phenomenologist, Rosa claims that social acceleration is present on both an international and individual level. For instance, he states that for a government's credit rating to remain to same they must accelerate the rate of production. This logic of needing to speed up just to stay where you are is what Rosa terms dynamic stabilization. On the individual level, social acceleration has led to burnout, since the number of possible tasks and commitments far exceeds the amount of time available. This creates a constant pressure to control, parameterise, and optimise every moment, leading individuals to feel perpetually overwhelmed and incapable of keeping pace, an experience he calls frenetic standstill.

He regarded these elements as both physical and spiritual forces within the human being. Each life essential was presented as corresponding to one of these principles, with air linked to breathing, fire to sunlight, water to purification, earth to nourishment, and spirit to movement and mental exercise. When these forces fell out of balance, disease and disorder were believed to result. He also related these ideas to the concept of the five geometric Platonic solids, which symbolized the pure forms of the elements. For Parkyn, this ancient framework demonstrated that true health depended on harmony between the visible and invisible forces of life. Dr. Parkyn was the first to identify and classify what he called the "suggestible somnambule," a term he introduced to describe individuals who live in a state of partial consciousness, neither fully awake nor fully asleep, yet unusually open to suggestion. The word somnambule, taken from the French for "sleepwalker," referred not to literal sleepwalking but to a psychological condition in which the mind remains active while reason and judgment are subdued.

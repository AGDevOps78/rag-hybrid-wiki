Hence, the count of electrons each shell can contain are 2, 8, 18, 32, and so forth. A shell is represented by a row on the periodic table.[68] The simplest atom is ordinary hydrogen, which consists of one proton and one electron. In its minimum energy ground state, the electron occupies the first shell, designated K. This shell has one subshell designated 1s. The second element, helium, has two protons, two electrons, and usually two neutrons. The second electron occupies the same orbital as the first, completing the shell with spin-paired electrons. For lithium there are three electrons, so one needs to occupy an orbital in the second shell, designated L. Per the Aufbau principle, the third electron occupies the next lowest available energy subshell, which is 2s.

During the Ming period ( As in China, marriage matches in Japan were expected to serve the ie, or the larger family, by perpetuating the family line, adding new adults to the family's labor force, forming alliances with other families, offloading female dependents. Almost all people were expected to marry and produce children. Marriage generally occurred between a small group of families of the same social class. When choosing spouses, families considered economic status and political views. Brides and grooms, and their families, were considered equals. Romance in marriage was discouraged, as it was thought to undermine filial piety. According to one proverb, "Those who come together in passion stay together in tears." Sexual gratification was more acceptable outside of one's marriage, through concubines, geishas, or "pleasure districts".

The network of scholars and practitioners that emerged from the school carried its influence into medicine, education, and the New Thought movement, making it one of the foundational institutions in the early development of American psychological science. The Chicago School of Psychology was widely recognized as the "parent school" of suggestive therapeutics in America. Under Parkyn's supervision, many of its graduates went on to establish affiliated schools throughout the United States and Canada that operated under the Chicago School's curriculum.

A typical example of a unimolecular reaction is cis–trans isomerization, in which the cis form of a compound converts to the trans form or vice versa. In a typical dissociation reaction, a bond in a molecule splits (ruptures) resulting in two molecular fragments. The splitting can be homolytic or heterolytic. In the first case, the bond is divided so that each product retains an electron and becomes a neutral radical. In the second case, both electrons of the chemical bond remain with one of the products, resulting in charged ions. Dissociation plays an important role in triggering chain reactions, such as hydrogen–oxygen or polymerization reactions. For bimolecular reactions, two molecules collide and react with each other. Their merger is called chemical synthesis or an addition reaction.

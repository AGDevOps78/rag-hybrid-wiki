Even among the working class, children were essential to help maintain farms and family businesses, so infertile women were often cast aside.[3] The invention of the plow widened the division of labor between men and women. Plowing required greater physical strength and was less compatible with childcare, and therefore became associated with male labor. The belief, therefore, that men contributed more to a household's resources led to the proliferation of the dowry. The increase in warfare between emerging societies also raised men and lowered women in the social hierarchy — devaluing women and contributing to the idea that their presence in a family needed to be compensated in some way. Over time, in many societies, women became seen as the properties of their fathers or husbands.[3] Where women could no longer choose their own mates, men now needed to win over their fathers.[9] Among the aristocracy, marriage became the site of political intrigue. Marriages — often temporary — were used to codify treaties and solidify relationships between different cities and even empires. Factions of ruling circles fought over who had the right to legitimize marriage and bloodlines — arguments which often had serious historical ramifications. Wealthy men often had multiple wives (and occasionally, powerful women would have multiple husbands).

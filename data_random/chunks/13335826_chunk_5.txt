The InChI Trust funds the development, testing and documentation of the InChI. Current extensions are being defined to handle polymers and mixtures, Markush structures, isotopologues and isotopomers,[18] reactions,[19] organometallics, and nanomaterials,[20] and once accepted by the Division VIII Subcommittee will be added to the algorithm. The InChI Trust has developed software to generate the InChI, InChIKey and other identifiers. The release history of this software follows.[21] The InChI has been adopted by many larger and smaller databases, including ChemSpider, ChEMBL, Golm Metabolome Database, and PubChem.[22] However, the adoption is not straightforward, and many databases show a discrepancy between the chemical structures and the InChI they contain, which is a problem for linking databases.[23]

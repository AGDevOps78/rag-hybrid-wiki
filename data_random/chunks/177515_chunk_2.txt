Molecular engineering efforts may include computational tools, experimental methods, or a combination of both. Molecular engineering was first mentioned in the research literature in The discovery of electrically conductive properties in polyacetylene by Alan J. Heeger in Molecular design has been an important element of many disciplines in academia, including bioengineering, chemical engineering, electrical engineering, materials science, mechanical engineering and chemistry. However, one of the ongoing challenges is in bringing together the critical mass of manpower amongst disciplines to span the realm from design theory to materials production, and from device design to product development. Thus, while the concept of rational engineering of technology from the bottom-up is not new, it is still far from being widely translated into R&D efforts. Molecular engineering is used in many industries. Some applications of technologies where molecular engineering plays a critical role: Molecular engineers utilize sophisticated tools and instruments to make and analyze the interactions of molecules and the surfaces of materials at the molecular and nano-scale. The complexity of molecules being introduced at the surface is increasing, and the techniques used to analyze surface characteristics at the molecular level are ever-changing and improving.

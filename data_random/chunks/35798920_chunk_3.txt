Factions of ruling circles fought over who had the right to legitimize marriage and bloodlines — arguments which often had serious historical ramifications. Wealthy men often had multiple wives (and occasionally, powerful women would have multiple husbands). Women would plot to overthrow their own husbands or sons-in-law in order to install their own male relatives to power, and sometimes wielded significant influence over their own men.[3] Generally, slaves were forbidden from having their own wives or households.[3] The primary purpose of marriage was to have more children and descendants of the family, and to legitimize inheritance rights.[10] Marriages were typically arranged by parents.[11] Among commoners, marriages were typically informal affairs. A couple was considered married once they had established a household together, and marriage did not require any special license or state approval. Propertied families, however, typically drew up prenuptial contracts for their own benefit.[12] Among the lower classes, husbands and wives worked together to manage businesses. Among the upper classes, women usually did not work outside the home, and instead supervised the servants of the household and her children's education.[13] Generally, women made more family decisions and controlled more of the home than in other ancient societies. Women had control over most of their property and could serve as legal persons who brought cases to the court.[11] Men who married higher-status women often were forced to yield to significant pressure from their wives and fathers-in-law.[14] Even pharaohs frequently found themselves under control over their mothers.[10] Widows gained more legal freedom after their husbands' deaths -- notably, the ability to buy and sell land.[15] Marriage for pharaohs was a more politically treacherous affair. Pharoahs often had multiple wives, and upper-class men would often marry off their female relatives to pharoahs to solidify connections with the royal family and represent their interests.[14] In-laws frequently fought over inheritance rights.

Nevertheless, marriages were typically private affairs and ancient governments rarely got involved. Because women had the unique ability to bear a child with an "impure" bloodline, generally their sexual behavior was more strictly supervised, especially among the aristocracy. Some societies developed elaborate ideas about female sexual purity. Even among the working class, children were essential to help maintain farms and family businesses, so infertile women were often cast aside. The invention of the plow widened the division of labor between men and women. Plowing required greater physical strength and was less compatible with childcare, and therefore became associated with male labor. The belief, therefore, that men contributed more to a household's resources led to the proliferation of the dowry. The increase in warfare between emerging societies also raised men and lowered women in the social hierarchy — devaluing women and contributing to the idea that their presence in a family needed to be compensated in some way.

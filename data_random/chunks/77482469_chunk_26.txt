Some intersex variations can impact cognitive functioning or carry higher rates of neurodevelopmental conditions like autism or ADHD. Rates of autism symptoms are higher in intersex people. A survey of European intersex adults found a 9.1 percent prevalence, compared to 1 percent in the general adult population. This percentage differs depending on the particular intersex variation, with those with Klinefelter syndrome having the highest autism symptom prevalence of the variations measured. Intersex people have no significant difference in intelligence quotient compared to endosex people. Other cognitive and neurodevelopmental differences depend on the particular variation. Females with Turner syndrome are more likely to be diagnosed with attention deficit disorder, can struggle with social competence in both childhood and adulthood, and can have visuospatial deficits that negatively impact math ability.

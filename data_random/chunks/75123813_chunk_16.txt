The HPA axis is referred to as one of the primary hormonal stress systems. Research looking at stress and social buffering in development consistently shows that parents play a role in shaping HPA axis function, which is evidenced in part by how early social deprivation may later result in long-term dysfunction of stress reactivity. Social buffering effects have also been observed when a child is exposed to a threatening event. The presence of a parent during such a time can lower or completely block the activation of the HPA axis. Additional support for the social buffering hypothesis and social neuroscience involves fear conditioning. The amygdala has been recognized as an important part in the process of fear learning, and research has shown that children have reduced amygdala activity when around a parent. In addition to this, greater connectivity between the prefrontal cortex and amygdala was observed when a child viewed their mother compared to a stranger. The prefrontal cortex is involved in emotional processes, so communication between the two brain areas indicates that parents play a large role in emotional regulation and provide neurological support for social buffering.

In ancient Greece, the philosophers Leucippus and his student Democritus proposed that all matter consists of indivisible units called atomos. .A similar idea developed in ancient India with Kanāda of the Vaiśeṣika school, who proposed indivisible particles (paramāṇu), while later Buddhist Abhidharma traditions also articulated atomistic views emphasizing momentary and causally dependent material phenomena. Although these early concepts differ fundamentally from the modern scientific understanding of atoms, both Greek and Indian philosophical traditions included speculative ideas that matter is composed of extremely small, indivisible units, developed independently of the experimental and quantitative framework later used in modern atomic theory. Adjusting these figures, in the grey powder there is about 13.5 g of oxygen for every 100 g of tin, and in the white powder there is about 27 g of oxygen for every 100 g of tin. 13.5 and 27 form a ratio of 1:2. Dalton concluded that in the grey oxide there is one atom of oxygen for every atom of tin, and in the white oxide there are two atoms of oxygen for every atom of tin (SnO and SnO2).

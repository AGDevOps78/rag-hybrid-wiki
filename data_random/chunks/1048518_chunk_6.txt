Supramolecular metallocycles and metallacrowns are related components.[37] Common metallocycle shapes in these types of applications include triangles, squares, and pentagons, each bearing functional groups that connect the pieces via "self-assembly."[38] Many supramolecular systems require their components to have suitable spacing and conformations relative to each other, and therefore easily employed structural units are required.[39] Supramolecular chemistry per se has found few applications, but underpins some useful phenomena.[41] The concepts of supramolecular chemistry can inspire the design of catalyst. Non-covalent interactions influence the binding reactants.[42] Design based on supramolecular chemistry has inspired the design of functional biomaterials and therapeutics.[43] Supramolecular biomaterials afford a number of modular and generalizable platforms with tunable mechanical, chemical and biological properties. These include systems based on supramolecular assembly of peptides, host–guest macrocycles, high-affinity hydrogen bonding, and metal–ligand interactions. A supramolecular approach has been used extensively to create artificial ion channels for the transport of sodium and potassium ions into and out of cells.[44] Supramolecular interactions influence drug-target binding. In the area of drug delivery, supramolecular chemistry could provide encapsulation and targeted release mechanisms.[45] In addition, supramolecular systems have been designed to disrupt protein–protein interactions that are important to cellular function.[46] Supramolecular interactions have been proposed to detect analytes.[47]

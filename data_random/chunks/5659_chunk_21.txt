For lithium there are three electrons, so one needs to occupy an orbital in the second shell, designated L. Per the Aufbau principle, the third electron occupies the next lowest available energy subshell, which is 2s. This process continues, with successive electrons being placed in the next available lowest energy orbital. Several terms are commonly used to characterise the general physical and chemical properties of the chemical elements. A first distinction is between metals, which readily conduct electricity, nonmetals, which do not, and a small group, the metalloids, having intermediate properties and often behaving as semiconductors. Besides being different by physical properties as substances, metallic and nonmetallic elements have different chemical properties as kinds of atoms: metallic elements form simple cations, whereas nonmetallic elements (and weak metals) form simple anions and oxoanions. An exception is noble gases, which do not form simple anions, whereas oxoanions are known for xenon. However, some metallic elements in higher oxidation states form oxoanions as well.

A placebo-controlled study found that administering intranasal oxytocin enhanced facial attractiveness of a romantic partner while viewing a photograph, as compared to an unfamiliar face. The effect was also measured using fMRI, which found enhanced brain activity in reward areas like the VTA and NAc. Another fMRI study found dopamine-rich genetic expression of an oxytocin receptor gene in the left VTA, and the left VTA has also been found active in response to facial attractiveness. In humans, circulating oxytocin levels have been associated with higher levels of interaction between partners, and also predicted which couples would still be together 6 months later. Anna Machin calls the combination of oxytocin and dopamine the "glue" which makes the early stages of a relationship possible. The role of oxytocin in human behavior is varied and complex.

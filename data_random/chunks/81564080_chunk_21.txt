Beyond its clinical instruction, the Chicago School developed numerous practical innovations and methods that contributed to the growing scientific understanding of mental influence. Dr. Parkyn experimented with new instruments, tested emerging technologies, and refined therapeutic procedures in ways that broadened the applications of suggestion in medicine and psychology. Parkyn was the first physician to use the phonograph as a tool for delivering therapeutic suggestions. He would record repeated affirmations and curative phrases onto wax cylinders, allowing patients to listen to them regularly and even use them at home as part of their treatment. This method was designed to reinforce mental conditioning through consistent exposure to spoken suggestions. Parkyn also found that the vibrations of the recorded voice attuned directly to the vibrational frequency of the patient carried strong psychological influences that acted directly on the involuntary mind.

AI developers have argued that such training is protected under fair use, while copyright holders have argued that it infringes their rights.[101] Proponents of fair use training have argued that it is a transformative use and does not involve making copies of copyrighted works available to the public.[101] Critics have argued that image generators such as Midjourney can create nearly-identical copies of some copyrighted images,[102] and that generative AI programs compete with the content they are trained on.[103] As of Getty Images has sued Stability AI over the use of its images to train Stable Diffusion.[104] Both the Authors Guild and The New York Times have sued Microsoft and OpenAI over the use of their works to train ChatGPT.[105][106] A separate question is whether AI-generated works can qualify for copyright protection.

A zonotope is a convex polytope that can described as the Minkowski sum of a finite set of line segments in R d {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{d}} or, equivalently as a projection of a hypercube. Zonotopes are intimately connected to hyperplane arrangements and matroid theory. The Minkowski sum of a finite set of line segments in R d {\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{d}} forms a type of convex polytope called a zonotope. More precisely, a zonotope Z {\displaystyle Z} generated by the vectors w 1 , . . . , w n ∈ R d {\displaystyle w_{1},...,w_{n}\in \mathbb {R} ^{d}} is a translation of Z = { a 1 w 1 + ⋯ + a n w n | 0 ≤ a j ≤ 1 for all j } = W   n , {\displaystyle Z=\{a_{1}w_{1}+\cdots +a_{n}w_{n}|\;0\leq a_{j}\leq 1{\text{ for all }}j\}=\mathbf {W} \, ^{n},} where W {\displaystyle \mathbf {W} } is the d × n {\displaystyle d\times n} matrix whose j'th column is w j {\displaystyle w_{j}} . The latter description makes it clear that a zonotope is precisely the translation of a projection of an n-dimensional cube.

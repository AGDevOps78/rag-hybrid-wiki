The target is moved around to break the reflex that normally holds the covered eye in the correct vergence position. The target is held steady before the eye is uncovered. The eye may flick quickly from being wall-eyed or cross-eyed to its correct position. If the eye moved from out to in, the person has esophoria. If it moved from in to out, the person has exophoria. If the eye did not move at all, the person has orthophoria. Most people have some amount of exophoria or esophoria, which is normal. If the uncovered eye also moved vertically, the person has hyperphoria (if the eye moved from down to up) or hypophoria (if the eye moved from up to down).

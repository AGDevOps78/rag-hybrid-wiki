When smoked, synthetic cannabinoids undergo pyrolysis, producing thermal degradants with varying pharmacological activities. Some of these by-products retain high affinity for CB1 receptors and may surpass the parent compounds in efficacy, further stimulating CB1 receptor-mediated responses. Synthetic cannabinoids can also interfere with the metabolism of other drugs, leading to potential drug-drug interactions. This interference could potentially alter the pharmacokinetics of co-administered substances, posing great health risks.

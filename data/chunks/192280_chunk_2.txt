Binocular interaction occurs when there is an interaction between the two eyes, which causes vision with both eyes to be different than with one eye alone. Vision can be better (binocular summation) or worse (binocular inhibition). In binocular summation, the signals from both eyes reinforce each other  so that visual acuity, contrast sensitivity, flicker sensitivity, and brightness sensitivity improve. Maximum binocular summation occurs when the sensitivities of each eye are equal. Differences in sensitivity decrease the effect of binocular summation. The effect of binocular summation decreases with age. In binocular inhibition, vision with both eyes is worse than with one eye. This can occur with strabismus[broken anchor] or a lazy eye, because the weaker eye interferes with the stronger one.

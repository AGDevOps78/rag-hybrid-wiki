Concepts such as phase transitions, self-organization, and stochastic fluctuations, traditionally studied in inanimate systems, have become central for understanding biological systems.[4] Biological physics has evolved from an interdisciplinary curiosity into a central part of modern physics.[2] Researchers emphasize that studying life offers opportunities to discover new organizing principles of matter and information.[4] The National Academies report and commentary from the American Physical Society call for expanded funding, interdisciplinary training, and infrastructure to accelerate progress.[5]

If the eye did not move at all, the person has orthophoria. Most people have some amount of exophoria or esophoria, which is normal. If the uncovered eye also moved vertically, the person has hyperphoria (if the eye moved from down to up) or hypophoria (if the eye moved from up to down). Such vertical phorias are quite rare. It is also possible for the covered eye to rotate in its orbit, known as cyclophoria, which is rarer than vertical phorias. The cover-uncover test may be used to determine the direction of deviation in cyclophorias.[10] The cover-uncover test can also be used for more problematic disorders of binocular vision, the tropias. In the cover part of the test, the examiner looks at the first eye while covering the second. If the eye moves from in to out, the person has exotropia.

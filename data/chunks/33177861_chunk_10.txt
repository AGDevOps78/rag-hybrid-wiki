also use electrophoresis to separate csncs from impurities. multiple evidence shows that csncs can be purified effectively by means of gel-electrophoresis techniques.[47][48][49] however, as purification of csncs via gel-electrophoresis is highly time-consuming, recently, nano-scientists are shifting towards more advanced free-flow electrophoresis (ffe)[50] and electrophoretic deposition (epd) techniques.[51][52] one of the most important properties of core–shell semiconducting nanocrystals (cssncs) is that their cores, which are quantum dots, fluoresce, which is important in their biomedical and optical applications.[53] the shells are highly modular, and thus the bulk properties, such as solubility and activity of the cssncs can be changed. the properties desired of cssncs when using them for biological applications include high quantum yield, narrow fluorescence emission, broad absorption profile, stability against photobleaching, 20 second fluorescent lifetime, and high brightness. high quantum yields mean that minimal energy will need to be put into the quantum dot to induce fluorescence.

Although these models are suitable for studying normal hair cycling, their anatomical differences limit the direct translatability of findings to humans. Large animal models offer closer approximation to human skin in terms of follicular structure and depth. They also allow for real-time monitoring of intrafollicular processes. Xenograft models are used for investigating androgen action and other mechanisms related to follicular delivery. In these models, human skin grafts are transplanted onto immunodeficient mice. These grafts retain the structural characteristics of human follicles and have contributed to the understanding of alopecia areata and androgenetic alopecia. Heightened interest in hair follicles stem from several dermatological abnormalities that relate to the follicles, including acne, androgenetic alopecia, and some skin cancers. Acne, or acne vulgaris, is a chronic skin condition in which oil and dead skin cells clog the hair follicles, leading to pimples, blackheads, and whiteheads.

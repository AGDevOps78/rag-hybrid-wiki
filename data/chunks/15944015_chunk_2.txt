The means for creating these analogies is intuition. This intuition is "the poet's peculiar quality in that it enables him to discover analogies which, hidden to reason, are yet the essentials of art".[7] The discovering of analogies is made possible by intuition. Marinetti believed that analogies had always existed, but earlier poets had not reached out enough to bring appropriately disparate entities together. By creating a communion of two (or more) seemingly unrelated objects, the poet pierces to the "essence of reality".[7] The farther the poet has to reach in terms of logical remoteness is in direct proportion to its efficacy. As analogy thus plays such an important role, it "offers a touchstone to gauge poetical values...: the power to startle. The artistic criterion derived from analogy is stupefaction".[7] While an ordinary person's vision is colored by convention and tradition, the poet can brush away this top layer to reveal the reality below.

Critics argue that online platforms are already subject to legal obligations to address child sexual abuse material and contend that the bill would create a notice-and-takedown mechanism that could be exploited by bad actors. They also maintain that the Act would apply to an overly broad range of services, including cloud storage providers and email platforms. Opponents have argued that the legislation could undermine end-to-end encryption by exposing providers to liability for content they cannot access. These groups emphasize that end-to-end encryption protects all users, including children and other at-risk populations, from surveillance and abuse. Although the bill contains some degree of protection for end-to-end encrypted services, critics contend that this provision is insufficient, as providers could still face costly litigation for providing them. Senator Wyden has also expressed concern about the Act, stating that while he is open to reforms to Section 230, the legislation could incentivize companies to proactively scan user communications.

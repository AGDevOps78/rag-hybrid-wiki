Where media remains a powerful information source for political information, it can create political bias in the informational representation of political actors  and policy issues. An example of quantification of political bias in the media is a propaganda model, a concept introduced by Edward S. Herman and Noam Chomsky. It is a political economy model, looking at the "manufacturing" of political policies through the manipulation of mass media. This model further looked at the capital funding of media outlets and their ownership, which often relates to political ties. A different quantification of political bias compares media positions relative to the median voter, where a Political bias in the media is also discussed, showing how social leaders discuss political issues. To determine the presence of political bias, agenda determination is used. Agenda determination is designed to provide an understanding of the agenda behind the presentation of political issues and attempt to determine political bias that is present.

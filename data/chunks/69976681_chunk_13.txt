The molecular structures were investigated with the aid of IR spectroscopy. It was shown that the electrophilic boron atom reacts with the less polar C–H bond. The carbon represents the partially negative (nucleophilic) part of the bond. A heterolytic bond cleavage of the C–H bond occurs, which formally leads to a carbanion (CH−3) and a proton (H+). The carbanion binds to the electrophilic boron atom of (B12X11)−, forming the dianion (B12X11CH3)2−, while the H+ ion remains electrostatically bound to this dianion in the gas phase. The overall singly-charged ion (B12X11CH3)2− (H)+ is a Brønsted acid.

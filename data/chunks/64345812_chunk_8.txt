Meanwhile, GPEs conduct ions mainly in the solvent, which acts as plasticizer.[69] The solvent acts to increase the ionic conductivity of the electrolyte as well as soften the electrolyte for improved interfacial contact. The matrix of GPEs consist of a polymer network swollen in a solvent that contains the active ions (e.g., Li+, Na+, Mg2+, etc.). This allows for the composite to contain both the mechanical properties of solids and the high transport properties of liquids. A number of polymer hosts have been used in GPEs, including PEO, PAN, PMMA, PVDF-HFP, etc. The polymers are synthesized with increased porosity to incorporate solvents such as ethylene carbonate (EC), propylene carbonate (PC), diethyl carbonate (DEC), and dimethyl carbonate (DMC).[70][71][72] Low molecular weight poly(ethylene glycol) (PEG) or other ethers or aprotic organic solvents with high dielectric constant like dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO) can also be mixed the SPE matrix.[73][74] UV and thermal cross-linking are useful ways to polymerize in-situ the GPE directly in contact with the electrodes for a perfectly adherent interface.[75] Values of ionic conductivity on the order of 1 mS/cm can be easily achieved with GPEs, as demonstrate the numerous research articles published.[76] Emerging subclasses of QSSEs use matrix materials and solvents. Ionogels, for example use ionic liquids as a solvent that has improved safety including non-flammability and stability at high temperatures.[68][77] Matrix materials in ionogels can vary from polymer materials[78] to inorganic nano-materials.[66] These matrix materials (as with all QSSEs) provide mechanical stability with a storage moduli up to 1 MPa or higher.

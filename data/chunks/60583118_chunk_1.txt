Academic conceptions of moral emotions have changed in recent years. A large part of moral emotions is based on society's interpretation of things. While it is true that many of these emotions are based on the absolute truths of morality, this is only but a part of what moral emotions are about. The full spectrum of what moral emotions entail also includes emotions based on the narratives of what people teach. Much of this leads people to make their own choices in life, through a process formally known as "moral decision-making". This is something that influences people every day, without most people ever even realizing it. The study of morality through philosophy dates back at least as far as Plato and Aristotle, who believed morality derived from moral reasoning and focused their studies there at the expense of moral emotions. The emotive side of morality, worked by later philosophers such as Adam Smith in his The Theory of Moral Sentiments and David Hume has been looked upon with disdain, as subservient to the higher, rational, moral reasoning.

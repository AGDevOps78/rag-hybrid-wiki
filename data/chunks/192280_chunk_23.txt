Stereograms have been and continue to be widely used in depth perception research, for entertainment, and for education. Stereograms can be made by hand, by drawing with a computer program, or by taking pictures with one or two still cameras (stereo camera) or video cameras.[23][24][25] The geometry used to design the correct disparities for these images is described in epipolar geometry and computer stereo vision. For natural scenes, the recordings for a stereogram are usually made from observation positions that are as far apart as the distance between the two eyes. In macro photography, this distance must be smaller to obtain a natural depth effect, and in a scout stereogram[broken anchor] it must be larger. Another special stereogram is based on the movement of the moon relative to the sun (moon stereogram).

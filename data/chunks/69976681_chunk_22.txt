Generation of exotic compounds for fundamental chemical research: Reactions with (B12X11)− anions enable the preparation of a variety of exotic compounds that are of interest for fundamental chemical research. These include, for example, noble gas-containing molecular anions    or highly charged cluster ions  that are usually not synthetically accessible. There has been some research on noble gas-containing molecular anions, but most of it is purely theoretical. In contrast, noble gas-containing anions of the type (B12X11 − NG)− (NG = noble gas) can actually be prepared experimentally, not only at temperatures near absolute zero (0 Kelvin) as required for weakly bound complexes, but also at room temperature (298 Kelvin). Moreover, they are comparatively robust against attack by nucleophiles such as water. At room temperature, small amounts of nucleophiles such as water can hardly be avoided in vacuum instruments and tend to decompose noble gas-containing reaction products by substitution reactions. However, this problem does not play a major role in the case of the noble gas-containing anions of the type (B12X11 − NG)− described here. Generation of novel substances as prototypes for potential drugs: (B12X11)− ions can be directly bound to larger organic structures to produce entirely new boron-containing organic molecules in this way.

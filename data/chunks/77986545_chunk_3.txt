Phase-shift keying (PSK) is a digital modulation scheme that conveys data by changing (modulating) the phase of a reference signal (the carrier wave). The signal is impressed into the magnetic field x,y area by varying the sine and cosine inputs at a precise time. It is widely used for wireless LANs , RFID and Bluetooth communication. Orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM) is a digital multi-carrier modulation scheme. OFDM conveys data on several parallel data channels by incorporating closely spaced orthogonal sub-carrier signals. OFDM is a favorable communication scheme in underwater acoustic communications thanks to its resilience against frequency selective channels with long delay spreads.[3][4][5] Continuous phase modulation (CPM) is a modulation technique, which is a continuous phase shift, where the phase of the carrier signal varies over time and avoids abrupt changes between successive symbols. This smooth phase trajectory reduces spectral side lobes.[6] Reducing spectral side lobes increases the spectral efficiency of CPM and enables it to transmit data within a narrower bandwidth.
